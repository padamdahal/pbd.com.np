Practical Guide: Using BS & AD Dates on Official Nepali Documents

Accurate BS–AD conversion prevents verification delays across government portals, passport applications, and academic records in Nepal.

e-Passport, NID & Lok Sewa Forms

Online government portals—including the Department of Passports (e-Passport), National ID (NID), and Lok Sewa Aayog—typically require ISO YYYY-MM-DD dates (for example, 2026-11-21 AD or 2083-08-05 BS), whereas bank KYC and embassy forms often request DD/MM/YYYY.

Always cross-check your converted AD date against your SEE/SLC certificate and citizenship before submitting, especially if your birthday falls between April 13 and April 15 where the BS year rolls over.

Old Citizenship Certificates (Nagarikta)

Older handwritten citizenship certificates often list only the holder's age on the issue date—for example, "Age 16 on 2065-03-10 BS"—without an exact birth date.

To estimate the BS birth year, subtract the stated age from the BS issue year (2065 − 16 = 2049 BS), then confirm the exact month and day from your birth registration (Janma Darta) or academic transcript before applying for an e-Passport or NID.

Why 1-Day Boundary Differences Occur

Each Bikram Sambat month begins at the astronomical Sankranti—the exact moment the Sun enters a new zodiac sign measured in Nepal Standard Time (UTC+5:45). When a transit occurs close to midnight, older printed wall calendars occasionally assign the extra day to the preceding or following month.

For dates near month ends (days 29–32) prior to BS 2040 or after BS 2083, confirm legal deadlines against the officially gazetted Nepal Panchanga calendar.

Why is the Bikram Sambat (BS) calendar about 56.7 years ahead of the Gregorian (AD) calendar?

Bikram Sambat began in 57 BCE, traditionally attributed to King Vikramaditya, and its new year (Baisakh 1) falls in mid-April rather than January 1. As a result, a BS year is 57 years ahead of the AD year between mid-April and December, and 56 years ahead between January 1 and mid-April.

How do leap years work in Bikram Sambat (BS) compared to Gregorian (AD)?

Gregorian leap years add February 29 on a fixed four-year rule (except century years not divisible by 400). Bikram Sambat is a solar-sidereal calendar whose months begin on exact solar Sankranti transits, so month lengths range from 29 to 32 days and years naturally total either 365 or 366 days without a single fixed leap day.

Why do some BS to AD conversions differ by 1 day on older printed calendars?

Because each Bikram Sambat month starts when the Sun enters a new zodiac sign (Sankranti) calculated in Nepal Standard Time, a transit occurring near midnight can shift a month length by one day across different almanac publishers. Always verify critical historical dates near month boundaries against official government records or the Nepal Panchanga Nirnayak Bikas Samiti calendar.

Which date format should I use for Nepal e-Passport, NID, and Lok Sewa forms?

Online portals such as the Department of Passports (e-Passport), National ID (NID), and Lok Sewa Aayog generally expect ISO format (YYYY-MM-DD, for example 2026-11-21 or 2083-08-05), while printed banking, visa, and academic forms often use DD/MM/YYYY.
