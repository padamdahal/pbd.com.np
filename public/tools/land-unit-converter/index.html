How Nepal land measurement works

Nepal uses two traditional systems depending on region. In the hills and Kathmandu Valley land is measured in Ropani, Aana, Paisa and Daam. In the Terai plains the system is Bigha, Kattha and Dhur. Government records increasingly also show area in square metres, while property listings often quote square feet.

A Ropani is defined as a square of 74 feet on each side (5,476 sq ft). A Bigha is a square of 270 feet on each side (72,900 sq ft). Because the two systems share no common sub-unit, conversion always routes through square feet or square metres.

Frequently asked questions

How many square feet is 1 Ropani?

1 Ropani = 5,476 square feet (≈ 508.74 m²). It equals 16 Aana, 64 Paisa or 256 Daam.

How many square feet is 1 Aana?

1 Aana = 342.25 square feet (≈ 31.80 m²). Four Aana make one Ropani quarter; sixteen Aana make a full Ropani.

How many square feet is 1 Bigha?

1 Bigha = 72,900 square feet (≈ 6,772.63 m²). It contains 20 Kattha or 400 Dhur.

How do I convert Ropani to Bigha (or vice versa)?

1 Bigha ≈ 13.31 Ropani. 1 Ropani ≈ 0.0751 Bigha (about 1.50 Kattha). Enter any value above and the converter shows the exact equivalent in both systems.

Which system is used in Kathmandu?

Kathmandu Valley and most hill districts use the Ropani–Aana–Paisa–Daam system. Prices are commonly quoted per Aana.

Is the result on this tool official for land deals?

No. This converter uses the standard reference factors for planning and comparison. The area written on the Lalpurja and the cadastral map from the Survey Office (Napi Karyalaya) is the official figure for any registration or transfer.
